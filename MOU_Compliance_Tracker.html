State of the MOU
Day 58 of 60, two days to the deadline, and no article changed status: the counts hold at Breached 7, At Risk 6, Conditional 1. The window's defining event moved the centre of gravity back to the water. ADNOC said two of its vessels were attacked while transiting the Strait of Hormuz on the evening of 13 August; the UAE foreign ministry condemned it in the early hours of 14 August, attributed it to Iran and the IRGC, called it piracy and a breach of Security Council Resolution 2817, and Bahrain endorsed that reading. ADNOC put the running total at fifteen of its vessels attacked since February, the second incident in under a week; Iran neither claimed nor denied it (Arts 1, 2, 13). Tehran hardened in parallel: the IRGC's Khatam al-Anbiya joint command said no ship can transit without Iranian permission, Basij head Hossein Taeb said the strait is under Iranian management, and Rasoul Sanaei-Rad tied reopening to Washington honouring the interim deal. Lloyd's List Intelligence put total Hormuz transits down 18 percent week on week, outbound down 30 percent, and the UKMTO/JMIC advisory current to 11 August still holds the strait at SEVERE with active mine danger areas and unfinished clearance (Art 5). On the US side the signalling was of indefinite continuation rather than wind-down: Defence Secretary Hegseth said in Panama that the Navy can hold the blockade indefinitely by rotating ships, and the Wall Street Journal reported USS George Washington sailing to relieve USS Abraham Lincoln after more than 250 days, preserving dual-carrier coverage. CENTCOM posted nothing new, so the tally stays at 59 vessels redirected, three disabled, two boarded as of 12 August (Arts 4, 9). Treasury Secretary Bessent said further measures of unprecedented severity would be announced within about a week, which is the inverse of a termination schedule and puts the status-quo freeze's no-new-sanctions limb at acute risk in the final days (Arts 7, 9). The verification flagged last refresh is now closed out: the OFAC General Licenses index was read directly and there is no GL X2 and no successor oil authorisation, with a WilmerHale alert independently confirming GL X ran only 22 June to 7 July (Art 10). Diplomatically the picture is unchanged and contested — no round, venue, date or announced Witkoff travel; Al Jazeera assessed the track as deadlocked with the Iran-Oman Hormuz channel the only live negotiation; the Anadolu-versus-Reuters conflict over whether an extension was agreed remains unreconciled, as does the 16-versus-17 August expiry discrepancy (Arts 3, 12). The official registers were otherwise clean negatives: no unblocking instrument, no Iran item anywhere on the Security Council's August programme across the deadline weekend, and nothing whatsoever from the IAEA, whose own Iran chronology still ends at June 2025 (Arts 6, 8, 11, 14).
