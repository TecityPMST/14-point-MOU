State of the MOU
On Day 55 of 60, with five days to the 16 Aug deadline, the memorandum is no longer being negotiated so much as argued over in reverse. Both principals said within 48 hours of each other that the track is not running: Araghchi said on 9 Aug that no negotiations are under way, only messages through intermediaries, and that talks cannot resume until US violations are remedied, while Trump said the US is low-keying it and only semi-negotiating (Art 3). What has replaced substance is a reparations argument — Tehran demanding compensation for war damage, Washington answering on 10–11 Aug that Iran should pay the United States for 50 years of damages. That inverts Article 6, whose premise is US-enabled financing of Iranian reconstruction, and moves it from Conditional to At Risk / Contested: the only status change this run, and the first time both governments have publicly asserted the other owes it money. The ceasefire limb had its worst window since signing. UNIFIL counted 455 Israeli projectiles into south Lebanon across 7–9 Aug with 97 airspace breaches, 229 of them on 8 Aug alone — a new highest single-day total since 21 Jun, displacing the 113 logged last run — plus an air strike on Al Mansuri and tank rounds landing about 200 metres from a UN position. At sea a missile struck an ADNOC-linked tanker inside the strait on 8 Aug, logged officially as UKMTO incident 108-26, drawing objections from the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Jordan and the GCC Secretary-General, and a Houthi drone hit Aramco's Jazan refinery on 9 Aug (Arts 1, 2). The one mitigating fact is narrow: CENTCOM's own release index shows no announced US or Israeli strike inside Iran across 7–11 Aug. On the strait the mechanism now contradicts the obligation. Iran and Oman agreed route coordinates — inbound through Iranian territorial waters, outbound through Omani — in an interim arrangement reported as 60 days and extendable, with a coordinated fee system under discussion five days before the toll-free obligation expires, and parliament's national security commission approved on 9 Aug the general framework of a bill barring US and Israeli vessels and fining violators up to 20% of cargo value (Arts 5, 2, 3). Volumes went the wrong way: 40 US-facilitated transits across 6–8 Aug, tankers at two to four a day, and weekend crossings falling 15 to 11 to 6 against a roughly 138-a-day baseline. Demining is contested at the highest level — Trump said on 11 Aug the Navy has cleared the strait and holds full control, while JMIC's 9 Aug advisory still lists active mine danger areas — and both are carried without resolution. The blockade tightened rather than lifted: CENTCOM's tally moved 49 to 53 to 55 vessels redirected inside one refresh interval, over 20 warships are described as enforcing it, and JMIC raised the Hormuz threat level to SEVERE, 25 days past the ~17 Jul full-lift deadline (Arts 4, 9). On the registers the movement was affirmative and restrictive: OFAC added 18 Iran-related SDN listings on 7 Aug in Treasury's eighth 2026 shadow-banking action, then nothing Iran-related on 8–11 Aug; the Iran general licence index verified on 11 Aug still enumerates no oil authorisation, putting Article 10 25 days in default; and no unblocking licence, release procedure or escrow mechanism touched the frozen funds (Arts 7, 10, 11). The IAEA's own channels failed on every endpoint for a fourth consecutive refresh, so Article 8 stays unverifiable rather than merely unmoved, and Iran's six conditions of 8 Aug contain no nuclear element at all (Arts 8, 13). The Security Council's August programme, which governs this whole window, still carries no Iran item and its Iran-tagged coverage stops on 30 Jul (Art 14). After 55 days there is still no US–Iran joint committee, working group or coordination cell of any kind, while Iran replaced its SNSC secretary on 9 Aug and named six senior military commanders on 10 Aug (Art 12). Counts: Breached 6, At Risk 7, Conditional 1 — the shift being one article moving out of the deferred column not because a deadline passed but because the obligation reversed direction.
